Healthcare · Commercialization Strategy · Korea, US & Japan

Organoid & 3D Cell Commercialization Strategy

Defined how a domestic biotechnology company could move beyond low-volume research-use-only sales and enter the GMP-grade regenerative-medicine ecosystem.

3 product categories 3-country analysis Double-digit market growth Phase 2 follow-on engagement

Business Question

What product, market and GTM model would create a defensible path from RUO demand to GMP commercialization?

Decision Criteria

  • Reproducibility and quality consistency
  • Regulatory readiness and validation support
  • Total cost of ownership
  • Switching cost and long-term account value

Key Evidence

  • Korea culture-media market projected at approximately 18% CAGR; Japan at 17%; US at 14%
  • Supplier changes become costly after clinical-stage validation
  • Equipment is purchased as quality-risk infrastructure, not only for productivity

Strategic Implication

Early adoption can create durable customer lock-in when consumables, equipment and validation support are designed as one system.

Recommendation Build a tiered RUO-to-GMP SKU architecture and bundle consumables with equipment. Use a turnkey solution model in the US and a process- and quality-led approach in Japan.
My contribution: Led market sizing, competitor and regulatory analysis, triangulated expert input, structured the commercialization logic and developed client-facing reports and presentations.
Source: Anonymized project analysis based on public market and regulatory data, expert interviews and internal synthesis.
Industrial Technology · Market Entry Strategy · Thailand

Industrial HVAC Market Entry Strategy

Evaluated whether a Korean industrial HVAC company should establish a local entity or enter through partners in Thailand's industrial and logistics market.

Industrial dehumidifier: ~USD 46M by 2031 Portable AC: ~USD 40M by 2031 Partner-led entry ASEAN expansion option

Business Question

Should the company invest in a local entity immediately, or validate demand through a lower-risk route first?

Decision Criteria

  • Market growth and industrial demand
  • Price-positioning gap
  • After-sales service capability
  • Fixed-cost exposure and partner access

Key Evidence

  • White space between Japanese premium brands and Chinese low-cost OEMs
  • Local A/S and technical support materially influence industrial procurement
  • Immediate entity setup creates disproportionate fixed-cost risk for an unvalidated market

Strategic Implication

A middle-tier positioning alone is insufficient; it must be paired with credible local service and distribution capability.

Recommendation Defer local-entity establishment, enter through a specialized distributor to validate demand, then expand into a local operating base once sales and service economics are proven.
My contribution: Structured market, competitor, tariff and channel analysis; compared entry models; and developed the phased GTM roadmap.
Source: Anonymized project analysis based on public market, trade and investment data and internal synthesis. Trade benefits remain subject to origin and eligibility requirements.
AgTech · Feasibility & Business Model · US Northeast

Controlled-Environment Agriculture Feasibility Strategy

Assessed the commercial feasibility of CEA in the US Northeast and identified the crop mix that best balanced operating stability and upside potential.

20×+ yield per area Lettuce payback: ~3–4 years Strawberry payback: ~3–6 years Dual-crop model

Business Question

Which production format and crop portfolio would provide a viable and resilient business model?

Decision Criteria

  • CAPEX and OPEX
  • Yield and crop-cycle productivity
  • Price volatility and demand risk
  • Payback period and operating complexity

Key Evidence

  • CEA lettuce yield per unit area can exceed open-field production by approximately 20 times
  • Lettuce provides faster and more stable cash generation
  • Strawberries offer premium-margin potential but wider execution risk

Strategic Implication

A single-crop model concentrates market and operating risk; a portfolio approach improves resilience.

Recommendation Use lettuce as the cash-flow anchor crop and strawberries as the premium growth crop within a dual-crop production portfolio.
My contribution: Led crop-level profitability simulation, production-scenario analysis, expert interviews and the business-feasibility framework.
Source: Anonymized project analysis based on public market data, expert interviews and internal financial modeling.

Workflow Transformation · Human-in-the-loop Validation

Research Acceleration & Synthesis System

Problem

Multilingual regulatory and competitive-intelligence work created long lead times and a higher risk of omission or unsupported synthesis.

Control Logic

  • Separate source extraction from interpretation
  • Cross-check claims against regulatory and market databases
  • Triangulate secondary data with interviews
  • Require final human approval before delivery
